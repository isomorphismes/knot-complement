# Project ideas

This file is the parking lot for the larger project. The point is to preserve the branches without forcing them all into the first implementation.

## A. Knot complements as places

The primary idea is literal traversal of a knot complement or another 3-manifold.

Do not merely draw a knot in ordinary 3-space. Put the camera inside the geometric manifold and let motion expose the topology and metric.

Candidate spaces:

- figure-eight knot complement (`m004`)
- Borromean-rings complement (`L6a4`)
- other small cusped hyperbolic manifolds from the SnapPy census
- closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds later

Possible visual aids:

- ideal tetrahedron faces and edges
- cusp cross-sections
- Dirichlet domains
- repeated recognizable objects to expose quotient identifications
- optional trails showing where the camera has been

## B. The eight Thurston geometries

Make the same small world in each geometry so the geometry, not the artwork, explains the difference.

The eight geometries:

1. Euclidean
2. spherical
3. hyperbolic
4. \(S^2 \times R\)
5. \(H^2 \times R\)
6. \(\widetilde{SL_2(R)}\)
7. Nil
8. Sol

Questions to make visible:

- What does "straight ahead" mean?
- How do parallel-looking paths behave?
- How does the horizon behave?
- How does distance feel?
- What happens to a row of equally spaced trees?
- What happens to a mountain range?
- What does turning around a loop do to orientation and apparent position?

A common auto-walk path would make side-by-side comparison much easier.

## C. Fractal traversal

Turn zoom imagery into navigation.

Possible starting material:

- cohomology fractals
- Cannon--Thurston maps
- Mandelbrot / connectedness-locus zooms
- limit sets of Kleinian groups

The interaction should eventually be more than a zoom slider: choose a direction, move, turn, and retain a sense of place.

## D. Familiar worlds in strange geometry

Use ordinary scenery as a ruler for unfamiliar geometry.

Examples:

- road through mountains
- forest
- tiled room
- repeated lamp posts
- village grid
- simple Zelda-like overworld
- large continuous landscape in the spirit of walking through a World of Warcraft zone

The mathematical geometry should deform the relationships among objects. The objects themselves can stay deliberately plain.

## E. Auto-walk / ride mode

A game mechanic is not required.

A useful mode is simply:

1. choose a space;
2. choose a path;
3. move continuously;
4. look around;
5. optionally stop and take manual control.

This keeps the project useful as a visualization even before it becomes a game.

## F. Four-dimensional experiments

Keep the 4D material nearby as a parallel source of interaction ideas:

- Jeff Weeks's *4D Maze*
- Jeff Weeks's *4D Draw*
- Jelle Vermandere's *4D Explorer*

The interesting question is not whether these belong to the knot-complement implementation, but what interaction techniques help a person build intuition for a space that cannot be seen all at once.

## G. Later algebraic / combinatorial worlds

Much less specified, but worth preserving:

- crossings and Reidemeister-like moves as traversable state changes
- cancellation / reduction processes
- spaces of transformations
- operator-composition worlds
- knot-theoretic state spaces

Do not let these delay the geometric fly-through work.
